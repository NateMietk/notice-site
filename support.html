Support

Questions, problems or ideas: noticeapp.support@gmail.com. A real person reads it; please allow a couple of days.

How Notice works

Each entry is one cycle: an evening, the night, and the next morning, so a page reads "Wednesday evening to Thursday morning". Until your morning ends (10am unless you change it in Settings) the app opens on the page whose morning is today. Everything saves as you enter it; there is no save button. Leave blank whatever you can't remember.

Apple Health isn't filling anything in

  • Open the Health app → your profile → Apps → Notice, and check the categories are allowed.
  • Sleep times come from a device that records sleep, such as an Apple Watch. Steps and workouts come from the iPhone or Watch.
  • "Minutes to fall asleep" needs your own lights-out time (type it, or use the Lights out stamp) and a recorded sleep.
  • Health values show a small heart. If you change one, your version stays.

Keeping a copy of your log

Settings → Your data → Export CSV saves everything to Files, Mail or anywhere the share sheet reaches. Your entries live only on your iPhone, so an occasional export is your backup. The same file imports back into the app.

The session brief

Practice tab → Make a session brief. Choose who it's for and which nights, read the summary the app wrote, then Make the PDF and share it. Everything on it comes from your own entries; nothing is sent anywhere unless you send it.

The lock

Settings → Care → "Require Face ID or passcode to open." If Face ID fails, your passcode works. Turning the lock off needs the app to be unlocked first.

Changing Day 1

Day 1 is the first night you logged. To match a paper log, pick a date in Settings → Your log; "Use my first logged night instead" goes back to automatic.

If things feel urgent

Settings → Care → "If things feel urgent" lists crisis lines and has room for your own therapist's number. Notice is a record, not a crisis service. In the US, call or text 988 any hour; elsewhere, findahelpline.com lists local lines. If you are in danger, call your local emergency number.

Notice does not provide medical advice. Bring what you notice to the people who care for you.
